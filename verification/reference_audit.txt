AH — arXiv:2609.18266v2
https://arxiv.org/html/2609.18266v2
Title, authors, version and date confirmed. Definition 2.1 and Theorem 2.2
are the symmetric displacement cover and reflecting-cell decomposition.
The attribution of the cover is appropriate; our refined parameter and
sampled Markov estimate are not attributed to that theorem. The v2 explicitly
supersedes AHIQP; the two references should not be presented as independent
sources of the same result.

DHKW06 — Mathematics of Operations Research 31 (2006), 147–153
https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/moor.1050.0169
Read the supplied journal PDF (EBSCO-FullText-10_14_2026.pdf). Metadata and
weighted power-sum approach agree. Its nonnegative maximization guarantee
must not be described as a multiplicative FPTAS for arbitrary minimization.
The short paper preserves this distinction and cites DHKW08 for the weak
range-relative extension.

DHKW08 — Mathematical Programming 115 (2008), 273–290
https://doi.org/10.1007/s10107-007-0175-8
Read supplied published PDF s10107-007-0175-8.pdf. Confirmed numbering:
Theorem 8 = compatible grid approximation, Lemma 9 = integral scaling,
Lemma 11 = local Lipschitz bound, Theorem 14 = constancy test/range lower
bound. Theorem 8 requires a multiple of Delta and sufficiently large grid
multiplier; Theorem 14 requires integer coefficients and k >= D*d_c.
The draft's q0=(D+1)*d_c*Delta satisfies that requirement. The grid variable
is unrelated to the power-sum exponent and requires no point counting.
The adaptation to a single grid and one integer approximation call is our
argument, not a verbatim theorem from DHKW08. The conditional error budget is now Lean checked (October 19 update);
the scaled-polynomial identity, integer coefficients, support and degree are
also now checked. The scaled feasible-set identification and coefficient binary-length bounds
are also checked, as are the scaled constraint binary-length bounds. Review the
grid/range-gap hypotheses and full running-time analysis independently; these
remain external to Lean. Rational row normalization now has checked feasibility
and encoding bounds.

Borwein–Erdelyi — Polynomials and Polynomial Inequalities, GTM 161 (1995)
https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4612-0793-1
Theorem 5.1.8 (p. 233) states the real univariate Markov inequality.
Affine changes of variable give both derivative bounds used in the proof.
Theorem 5.1.7 is the interior Bernstein bound. Section 5.2 treats higher
 derivatives, including Duffin–Schaeffer bounds at Chebyshev nodes; these
are not a direct replacement for our equally spaced integer samples and
even-part argument. The classical derivative estimates remain hypotheses
in Lean, not formalized conclusions.

Pierzchala — Mathematische Annalen 366 (2016), 57–82
https://doi.org/10.1007/s00208-015-1294-9
Theorem 1.1 restates classical univariate Markov. Theorem 1.4 (p. 59)
preserves the Markov property under polynomial maps of full generic target
rank. Its proof supplies constants depending on the set and map, but is not
a uniform bit-complexity theorem or an integer sampling theorem. We cite
it for continuous extensions, not for a low-rank integer FPTAS.

Piazzon–Vianello — Optimization Letters 13 (2019), 1325–1343
https://doi.org/10.1007/s11590-018-1377-0
Full text now inspected. Proposition 1 (p. 1327) converts norming to
range-relative optimization; its elementary proof also applies to finite
sets whenever the stated norming inequality holds. Proposition 2 and proof
(pp. 1328–1329) choose arbitrary feasible representatives in grid boxes,
using a Markov derivative bound. Proposition 3 (p. 1332) uses affine
normalization for general convex bodies with O((D^2/epsilon)^d) samples
for fixed dimension. Smooth-body improvements have additional geometric
hypotheses. These continuous results do not establish our integer-range
bound. Pinpoint citations replace the earlier abstract-only comparison.
