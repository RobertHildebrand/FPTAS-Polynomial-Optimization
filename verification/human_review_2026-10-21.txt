1. CHKM ENCODING AND THE UPPER SLACK BOUND — PASSES
Read the supplied published CHKM PDF, pp. 29–30, including the displayed estimates visually. Use F=(d+1)(tau+3), an upper bound on the per-inequality size INCLUDING the RHS; it is not total input size phi. The original row is integral after normalization, so CHKM's common denominator is 1. Its outward row 2a*x <= 2b+1 has slack 2s, not s.
The earlier estimates on p.29 give
 |v_j| <= (d+1) 2^[2(d+1)^2 F],
 2s_i(v) <= 2^[dF] + d(d+1) 2^[(d+2(d+1)^2)F].
These match the explicit hypotheses of chkm_encoding_translation. Do not rely on the last rough simplification printed on p.29: at d=2 its preceding expression does not imply that last inequality without an additional constant. Our Phi=5d^2 F+2 DOES suffice. For d=2, after dividing the slack estimate by 2, it is 2^(2F-1)+3*2^(20F)<4*2^(20F). For d>=3 the exponent margin absorbs the prefactors. The coordinate estimate is smaller as well.
P intersect Z^d is finite because P is bounded. Its nonempty integer hull is the convex hull of its vertices; affine slacks and coordinates extend the vertex bounds to every integer feasible point. Absolute coordinate bounds follow by the triangle inequality. Strict upper bounds survive finite convex combinations. An empty hull makes these conclusions vacuous. For d=1, a nonempty bounded interval defined by integral rows has endpoint magnitudes bounded by max |b_i| (nonzero integer denominators have magnitude >=1). This gives the stated much larger Phi bound directly. Rational denominator clearing includes b and has polynomial arithmetic cost.

2. COVER, CHAMBER ENUMERATION, AND LP ORACLE — PASSES
Every integer feasible point has all shifted slacks >=1/2 and <2^Phi, hence lies strictly inside Q. Q is bounded: its recession directions satisfy Av=0, while a nonempty bounded P has trivial kernel. This applies also when P itself is lower-dimensional. Nonzero row normals are necessary for the margin criterion and are explicitly removed in preprocessing.
The finite arrangement's full-dimensional chambers cover the interior of Q after taking closures. Internal boundary lattice points are covered; they need not avoid subdivision hyperplanes. Empty interior of Q therefore implies integer infeasibility.
Incremental insertion has at most R_d(H) retained cells at every stage. Each insertion makes at most two LP tests per retained cell. Tangencies and coincident hyperplanes create no missing full-dimensional children; duplicate boundaries do not invalidate the upper bound. The bound is O((H+1)R_d(H)) LP tests, versus at most R_d(H) integer-feasibility tests. Compression keeps the strongest lower and upper bound for each original slack. A straightforward implementation also spends polynomial bookkeeping time per test, absorbed by the bound.
For a weak cell C, the common-margin LP has constraints a_j*x+delta<=b_j and 0<=delta<=1. It is feasible iff C is nonempty. If feasible, it attains its bounded objective; here its feasible set is closed and bounded because x lies in Q. Nonempty interior is equivalent to a feasible positive common margin, using finitely many nonzero normals. Infeasible LPs must be discarded explicitly; the conditional Lean theorem assumes a feasible optimum and is not a statement that every query has one.
The integer rounding ceil(a-1/2)<=b_i-a_i*x<=floor(c-1/2) gives a subset with exactly the same lattice points, including when the rounded cell is empty. Its coefficient lengths are independent of ell.

3. RELATIVE-SLACK IFF AND ZERO-SLACK CASE — PASSES
For v=y-x, s_i(x+t v)=s_i(x)+t(s_i(y)-s_i(x)). Its minimum for |t|<=ell is s_i(x)-ell*|s_i(y)-s_i(x)|. Repeating at y proves both directions of the stated iff. The inequalities are weak because the relaxation is closed. If one slack is zero, the condition forces their difference to vanish. For positive slacks, dividing by the smaller gives the ratio <=1+1/ell. No continuous optimality assumption is used.

This supplies exactly CenteredMarkovBound D ell and IntervalMarkovBound (D/2) 0 ell^2 in SampledMarkov, and IntervalMarkovBound D 0 1 in the mixed reduction. These intervals have positive length in their applications, including D=1 (the contracted degree is then zero).
Nearest-integer distance <=1/2 yields W<=(b-a)/2+D^2 W/(2ell); ell>=D^2 gives W<=b-a. The centered even part h(t^2) has degree <=floor(D/2). Its derivative bound is 2 floor(D/2)^2 W/ell^2. The discrete inequality p(-1)>=p(0) bounds p(1)-p(0) by twice h(1)-h(0). This proves the displayed constant, including b=a and D=1. Feasible INTEGER progression samples justify the integer range. No bound on the continuous range over P is being substituted.
Negating f supplies the maximum statement, and f_min>=0 supplies Corollary 1.2. Boundedness supplies attained extrema and finite candidate sets, fulfilling the corresponding Lean hypotheses. Classical Markov remains imported, not newly formalized.

5. BIT COMPLEXITY AND FORMAL ENCODING INTERFACE — PASSES AS AN ALGORITHM ANALYSIS
With unary exponents, D<=O(phi), m,d<=phi, tau=O(phi), and Phi=O(d^3(tau+1)). The refinement has polynomial bit length even though its numerical value can be large. Thus H=O(m(Phi+2)ell), R_d(H)<= (1+H)^d, and (H+1)R_d(H) is (phi ell)^{O(d)}. Kannan's n^{O(n)} dimension dependence and polynomial dependence on encoding length yield the claimed 2^{O(d log(d+1))} poly(S_C) oracle cost; witness production can also be obtained by coordinate feasibility bisection within the proved finite coordinate bounds, with polynomial overhead. This uses an exact theoretical oracle, not a verified concrete solver.
Exact evaluation has O(<f>+D Phi+log(number of monomials+1)) intermediate bit length when each monomial is evaluated by repeated squaring and the results are summed. Threshold construction, integer gcd/lcm arithmetic, and LP serialization have polynomial bit costs on these lengths. Substitution of ell gives (phi D/epsilon)^{O(d)}. The LP count grows as epsilon^{-(d+1)/2}; the integer-query count grows as epsilon^{-d/2}, for fixed P,D,d.
Read the actual complete_lp_polynomial hypotheses. The input gives the required m,d, coefficient, denominator-size and degree-size bounds; normalized coefficients are controlled per entry by the original rational row payload. The total normalized matrix size need not be <=phi, and the argument does not require that. Dense row/list metadata in CellEncoding is an explicit encoding choice compatible with the manuscript's asymptotic convention. The constant 200 is attached to that formal schema, not asserted as a universal serialization constant in the paper. No implementation or operation-cost proof has been added to Lean by this review.

6. MIXED-INTEGER REDUCTION — PASSES
Checked the supplied DHKW published PDF: Lemma 9 is integral slice scaling; Lemma 10 uses downward barycentric rounding; Lemma 11 is the coefficient Lipschitz estimate. The paper's attribution and stated point of departure are correct.
If the mixed feasible set is nonempty, boundedness implies the continuous matrix has full column rank (a kernel direction would give an unbounded feasible line). Every vertex of a slice has d_c independent active rows, including lower-dimensional slices. Cramer's rule gives denominators dividing the corresponding nonzero determinant. The lcm over all such bases therefore supplies Delta uniformly in integral z. An empty feasible set is detected by the final grid feasibility test; if there are no nonzero bases at all, set the empty lcm to 1 (the bounded instance is necessarily empty).
Each nonempty slice is a bounded polytope. Caratheodory gives at most d_c+1 vertices, padded with repeated vertices/zero weights if needed, and a largest weight >=1/(d_c+1). These instantiate SliceData, with all integer coordinates fixed in the slice.
Upward rounding of the other weights loses at most d_c/k from that largest weight. With t=d_c(d_c+1)/k, all mu_i >= (1-t)lambda_i, so the residual weights define a point w in the same slice and y=(1-t)x+t w. The grid point is rational with denominator k Delta. The chosen k gives 0<t<=epsilon/(4D^2), and Markov gives error <=epsilon R/2. Grid optimization contributes another <=epsilon R/2. This works for R=0 and for empty feasibility; d_c=0 is separated.
Hadamard bounds each determinant's bit length by O(d_c(tau+log(d_c+1))). There are at most binom(m,d_c) of them. Repeated lcm via gcd has polynomial bit cost in the sum of those lengths. Scaling multiplies coefficients and RHS as stated, preserves degree/support, and raises polynomial coefficient lengths by O(D log Q). With unary D, this gives scaled size phi^{O(d_c+1)} and composed time (phi D/epsilon)^{O(d(d_c+1))}. The improvement is a coefficient-independent sufficient multiplier; it does not prove a better overall asymptotic runtime than DHKW.

7. DISCUSSION AND COMPARISONS — PASSES WITH STATED LIMITATIONS
The integer-hull vertex observation is correct: a distinct representative y in the same cell as a vertex v would make 2v-y feasible and contradict extremality. Thus every such vertex must itself be the cell's integer representative. The local-surrogate error 2delta+eta follows by two residual estimates and the local optimization guarantee. Low-rank and nonpolyhedral extensions are explicitly proposals; no new FPTAS is asserted for them.
Del Pia's published Proposition 3 and Section 6.2 give epsilon^{-k/2} box/MILP dependence and an epsilon-independent, k,p-dependent recursion tree. The manuscript's comparison distinguishes this from our LP enumeration overhead and does not claim a low-rank theorem.
Pak–Panova arXiv:1404.0653v3, Theorem 2.3, explicitly states L^{O(d log d)} for counting. The manuscript correctly calls this a comparison of available upper bounds, not a lower-bound separation or a practical speed claim. Its exact theorem numbering in the journal version remains unconfirmed here.
Piazzon–Vianello Propositions 1–2 do give the continuous range-relative mesh argument and arbitrary representatives from small boxes; Pierzchala Theorem 1.4 requires full generic target rank. The manuscript retains these hypotheses and does not use these continuous results as an integer sampling theorem.
